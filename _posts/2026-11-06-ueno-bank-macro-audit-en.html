A bank can appear sound until a closer examination changes what its loans are worth. The question at ueno bank is not just why the central bank stepped in, but what warning signs were visible earlier and how much the people responsible for oversight could actually know.

This is a PUBLIC-RECORD ASSESSMENT, not a certified audit or on-site bank examination. It draws on official statements, published legislation and attributed reporting. We do not have the full loan files, transaction records or board minutes.

AUDIT / 01An official decision, unanswered questions

Paraguay's central bank resolved on 2 November 2026 to start resolution proceedings for ueno bank and announced the decision the following day. It cited a solvency ratio below half the legal minimum after adjustments during a regularisation process.

Resolution is not, in itself, proof of fraud or of an intent to bring down the bank. It is a statutory process designed to address a failing institution and protect depositors. Sources refer to roughly 2.9–3 million customers; this figure must not be equated with the number of depositors who will permanently lose money.

What changed in the accounts, and when could the warning signs have been recognised?

[1][15][16]

AUDIT / 02Loan quality: the first financial X-ray

When a bank lends money, it records a claim on the borrower as an asset. But a loan with a face value of 100 million is not necessarily worth 100 million: repayment prospects, collateral and the borrower's finances matter.

ABC Color, citing supervisory records, reported loans large relative to some borrowers' demonstrated means and adjustments to provisions exceeding 1.6 trillion guaraníes. This does not mean that amount physically left the bank on one day; provisioning reflects expected or identified credit deterioration.

A complete examination would classify borrowers, test collateral, review related-party exposures and assess the entire book. A sample can reveal weaknesses without proving that every loan is impaired.

How much of the loan book was genuinely recoverable, and who checked?

[2][11]

AUDIT / 03Solvency: why one percentage cannot tell the whole story

The BCP officially says the ratio fell below half its statutory minimum. ABC Color reports a 5.56% ratio from the case documents. The full regulatory bridge showing the starting figures, each adjustment and the final calculation is not available in this public assessment.

Solvency relates regulatory capital to risk-weighted assets. Recognising previously understated credit losses can reduce capital. Without the detailed inputs, no single ratio can establish personal responsibility.

FIX SCR cut the national rating from AA-py to Dpy, explaining that material information had not previously been supplied by the bank. Establishing the date on which each fact became known is crucial.

Why had asset quality not been reflected earlier in the information available?

[1][2][4]

AUDIT / 04Liquidity: a separate alarm

Solvency measures capacity to absorb losses. Liquidity measures the ability to meet payments when due. A bank can run short of cash without having the same-sized accounting loss, and vice versa.

Press reporting based on the case file says ueno had difficulty replenishing roughly US$70 million in required reserves. That dollar figure cannot be added to guaraní-denominated credit provisions and labelled one combined loss.

An independent audit would need daily deposit movements, maturities, liquid assets and contingency-funding plans — none of which are fully available here.

Was the immediate problem liquidity, capital, or a combination?

[2][17]

AUDIT / 05Supervision did not begin in November

The central bank's October answers to Congress identify a supervisory note dated 5 June, a memorandum dated 24 July, an August 5 report and a corrective instruction dated 6 August. The responses also discuss related parties and a loan-portfolio sale.

It would therefore be incorrect to say that no warnings existed before November. But the public record does not establish that supervisors already knew the full later capital shortfall, or that every response was adequate.

A proper audit would map each alert to its receipt date, the responsible official, the deadline given, the bank's reply and the supervisor's follow-up tests.

Did the corrections reduce genuine risk, or merely improve how it appeared?

[3]

AUDIT / 06Corporate governance and the independence question

Reuters reported that Carlos Carvallo formerly headed Financiera ueno before becoming president of the BCP. The central bank said he recused himself from decisions involving the lender. Carvallo stepped down from his BCP role in November 2026.

Past employment alone is not misconduct. An independence review would ask for written recusals, delegation records, minutes and measures preventing informal influence.

Inside a bank, the board oversees risk, Risk and Compliance functions monitor and challenge it, and Internal Audit provides independent assurance. The internationally recognised Three Lines Model illustrates these roles without establishing any individual's guilt.

Which warnings reached the bank's board, and what happened next?

[5][12]

AUDIT / 07Following money trails without presuming offences

Related-company transactions and loan portfolio transfers require careful tracing. A transaction between affiliated companies is not automatically money laundering.

Understanding whether risk really moved outside the group requires contracts, source and destination accounts, beneficial owners, security arrangements and any cross-border transfers.

On 5 November prosecutors announced three criminal investigations encompassing allegations about supervision, public deposits and officials' conduct. Precautionary restrictions concerning 34 individuals and companies were reported to preserve assets; they are not criminal convictions.

Was new independent funding introduced, or did the same risk circle between entities?

[3][7][8][13]

AUDIT / 08Depositors: three different mechanisms

The BCP described an ordinary guarantee of up to 75 monthly minimum wages per depositor and bank, equivalent at the time to ₲228.3 million. On 4 November it outlined advances of up to 40 wages with deductions for overdue debts; on 5 November it detailed a first payment group with balances up to ₲10 million, no debts and an account at another institution.

These are different mechanisms, not three interchangeable guarantee ceilings. Legal coverage does not mean funds have already arrived, and sums above the limit are not automatically lost.

A citizen-centred review must measure actual payments, waiting times, outstanding balances, disputed claims and the effect on pensioners, workers and small businesses.

How many savers have actually regained access to their money?

[1][9][10][14]

AUDIT / 09One troubled bank is not automatically a system-wide collapse

The BCP described this as a case specific to ueno bank. An independent assessment of contagion would require updated data on deposits at other banks, system liquidity and interbank funding.

The regulator also reported temporary app disruptions at other banks amid unusually high demand for new accounts. That disruption does not, by itself, demonstrate that those banks are insolvent.

Macroeconomic risk is assessed with indicators, not rumours.

Which measures would show whether contagion has been contained?

[1][17]

AUDIT / 10The boundary between evidence and suspicion

Public documents already allow us to identify risk areas: credit valuation, solvency, liquidity, related-party exposure, governance and the operation of deposit guarantees.

They do not allow a complete statement of final losses, a full reconstruction of transactions or a finding about personal intent. Missing records could strengthen some hypotheses; they might also disprove others. A good audit must allow for both possibilities.

The evidence matrix below separates what is published, what is attributed to a source and what remains necessary to reach a reliable conclusion.

AUDIT / 11A response that puts citizens first

First, publish practical, measurable payment information: the number of depositors paid, amounts credited, time to access and a clear complaints process, including for people without digital access.

Second, commission an independent forensic reconstruction with lawful access to loan files, collateral, beneficial-owner records and board decisions. Recovering assets is not the same as rescuing shareholders.

Third, conduct an institutional review tracing each supervisory warning to the action it produced, while preserving due process and the rights of everyone concerned.

Who must account for decisions, how much money can be recovered and what must change?

THE EVIDENCE MAP — 12

  1. Which loans were reclassified, and why?
  2. What explains each capital adjustment?
  3. Which liquidity obligations were coming due?
  4. Where are Carvallo's written recusal records?
  5. Who received each supervisory warning?
  6. What did the board decide about related companies?
  7. Did portfolio sales actually transfer risk?
  8. Who knew the beneficial owners of counterparties?
  9. What did Internal Audit review before major loans were approved?
  10. How many depositors have actually been paid?
  11. How much public money can be recovered?
  12. What reforms would strengthen supervisory independence?

Not knowing everything is not permission to invent the rest.

A public-record review can already identify warning signs and important questions. A full forensic audit might establish how extensive the problems were. It might also rule out some theories. The severity of a crisis must be demonstrated, not presumed.
