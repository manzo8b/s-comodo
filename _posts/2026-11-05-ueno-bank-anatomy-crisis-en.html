Paraguay: anatomy of a banking crisis

From Financiera El Comercio to ueno bank: corporate power, supervisory warnings, contested transactions and the cost borne by citizens.

Before it became a digital bank, ueno was a finance company. Before it entered resolution, supervisors had raised concerns. And before the country learned the scale of the crisis, parliamentarians had already questioned lending to related companies.

On 2 November 2026, the Central Bank of Paraguay (BCP) decided to initiate resolution proceedings for ueno bank. It said balance-sheet adjustments had pushed the solvency ratio below 50% of the statutory minimum. Resolution is not itself a court judgment declaring bankruptcy. 1

On 5 November President Santiago Peña announced his resignation amid protests and intense political pressure. Resignation is not an admission of criminal responsibility. 9 10

The question running through this dossierWho knew what, when did they know it, and what did they do next?

01 / HistoryFrom finance company to bank: the transformation

The first correction matters: ueno bank did not start out as a bank or a cooperative. Its corporate origins lie in Financiera El Comercio S.A., established by public deed on 26 April 1976. 19

In 2021, Grupo Vázquez acquired control with approval from the BCP. The business subsequently took the Financiera ueno name. On 22 November 2023, by Resolution No. 22, Minutes No. 67, the BCP authorised its conversion into a bank. Banking operations began on 1 December. 20 21

On 28 June 2024 ueno bank absorbed Visión Banco in a merger authorised by the BCP. The bank's subsequent accounts say Visión contributed approximately ₲7.62 trillion of assets and ₲6.88 trillion of liabilities at the merger date. These figures describe the size of the transaction: they are not an allegation of missing money or proven losses. 22 23

In plain language

What is the difference between a finance company and a bank?

In Paraguay, a licensed finance company may also accept savings deposits, on demand or for fixed terms (Article 73 of Law 861). A bank can conduct additional activities, including operating bank current accounts (Article 40). Conversion requires new authorisation and checks: it is not merely a name change. 27

A question for the BCPWhich capital tests, risk controls and special conditions were reviewed in 2023 and during the 2024 merger, and how was ongoing compliance monitored?

02 / OwnershipWho actually controlled ueno bank?

The ownership structure extended beyond Paraguay. As of 19 March 2026, the bank's published shareholder register showed U Paraguay S.A. owning 98.25% of ueno bank. U Holdings S.à r.l., incorporated in Luxembourg, held 100% of U Paraguay. Grupo Vázquez owned 99.16% of U Holdings at that date. These figures are a March snapshot, not necessarily the position in November. 4

Hungarian lender OTP Bank was a minority investor. In June, a further investment was announced, intended to bring its share of the holding company to 9.99%. Investing in, or lending to, a bank does not by itself establish participation in contested transactions. 5

Federico Miguel Vázquez Villasanti was central to the group's economic control and was named in the prosecutor's case. Corporate influence does not automatically prove that one person ordered each loan. Investigators must distinguish ownership, management and payment execution. 3

Question for shareholdersWho ultimately benefited from the disputed loans, and who had the authority to approve or halt them?

03 / Forensic financeThe circle of money: did the risk really leave?

Prosecutors are examining loans and overdrafts extended to related companies in 2025–26, allegedly on inadequate terms and with deficient supporting documents. They also describe a transfer of a loan portfolio worth approximately US$74.4 million to a company allegedly funded indirectly by the bank itself. These are prosecution allegations, not proven findings. 3

A simple illustration

What does “circular financing” mean?

Imagine a bank selling old loans for 100 million. Another company buys them, which may be entirely legitimate. But suppose the buyer ultimately pays with money channelled through other companies that originally came from the seller bank. The payment comes back, but the lending risk may never have left the same economic group.

The 100 million is an invented teaching example, not a figure from ueno's transactions.

Selling loans is not inherently improper. The legal and prudential issue is whether the buyer was economically independent and whether risk genuinely transferred. The BCP's October replies already mentioned administrative examination of a potentially indirectly financed loan sale. 2 24

Forensic questionDid genuinely new capital enter, or did the same funds pass through several companies before returning to the bank?

04 / SupervisionThe controls existed. Why were they not enough?

The BCP produced supervisory documents in June, July and August 2026. In October it told Congress that certain related-party exposures had been corrected, while also reporting an ongoing administrative procedure relating to possible indirect financing of a portfolio sale. 2

External auditors, banking supervisors and parliament do different jobs. Auditors examine financial statements within their engagement; supervisors monitor prudential compliance; parliament scrutinises public institutions. A parliamentary report is not a loan approval. Nor does an audit of 2025 accounts automatically validate all 2026 transactions. 23

The central question is why exposures described as corrected in October were followed by such severe insolvency findings in November. Without complete records, institutional fault cannot simply be presumed.

Question for the BCPDid the corrections genuinely reduce economic risk, or largely change how risk appeared in the accounts?

05 / IndicatorsFrom AA−py to Dpy: what ratings can tell us

On 3 November, rating agency FIX SCR lowered ueno bank's domestic rating from AA−py to Dpy. It said previously provided audited, supervisory and management information did not reveal the deviations later identified by the BCP. This raises questions about the timeliness and completeness of disclosures. A rating is not a linear percentage loss measure. 6

06 / GovernanceWhat did the board know?

Lawyers representing three executives — Juan Manuel Gustale, Alejandro Gómez Abente and Diego Duarte — said their clients did not know certain borrowers were connected to the bank's controlling group. Their lawyer told ABC Color the position was recorded in June. It is a defence claim, not an adjudicated fact. 13

Boards can misjudge risks, knowingly take imprudent risks, or potentially engage in deliberate misconduct. Those are different factual and legal categories. Minutes, votes, risk reports and beneficial ownership checks are needed to tell them apart.

Question for the boardWho identified related parties, who approved each credit facility, and which warnings were recorded?

07 / ParliamentWhat did Congress know and do?

The Senate requested answers to 31 questions on lending limits, related parties and corrective measures in September. The BCP responded in early October, with cautions about banking secrecy. Receiving information is not approving the bank’s transactions. Nor does it prove that every legislator saw each underlying document. 14 2

Question for legislatorsAfter receiving the report, what hearings, documents or investigations did the responsible committees pursue?

08 / PoliticsSantiago Peña: business ties and resignation

Peña held shares in the Paraguayan company ueno Holding S.A.E.C.A., which should not be confused with Luxembourg's U Holdings S.à r.l.. Corporate information reported by ABC Color described a 1.58% stake valued at roughly ₲6.144 billion. Peña said he had disposed of the holding in March 2025. The underlying documents and disposal dates deserve direct review. 17 18

He resigned on 5 November amid protests and parliamentary pressure, saying his aim was to safeguard national stability and democratic institutions. Neither former holdings nor resignation alone prove criminal liability. 9 10

Conflict-of-interest questionWhich public decisions involved businesses linked to Peña’s former interests, and what disclosures, recusals or legal opinions are on record?

09 / AMLAnti-money laundering: trace funds, avoid assumptions

Related-party lending is not automatically money laundering. A separate financial-crime investigation must establish beneficial ownership, provenance of funds, economic purpose and any cross-border path. Prosecutors said they were evaluating possible assistance from Luxembourg and the United States. 3 12

Suspicious transaction reports are usually confidential: their existence or contents cannot be assumed without lawful evidence.

Follow-the-money questionFrom which accounts did the funds originate, who received them, and what evidence explains every subsequent transfer?

10 / Public impactPublic money and pension funds

Reuters reported that ueno administered around US$850 million in public funds. The IPS social security institution separately reported around US$355 million of exposure, equal to about 9% of its investment portfolio. Do not add these figures together: they may overlap, relate to different instruments and are not confirmed final losses. 9 8

Question about public moneyWho selected the bank for public deposits, under what concentration limits and using which risk assessments?

11 / DepositorsDepositors must come first

The BCP stated that ordinary deposit protection covers up to 75 monthly minimum wages per depositor per institution (about ₲228.3 million at the date of its notice). It also announced an advance of up to 40 minimum wages, subject to conditions and deduction of overdue debt. An earlier announcement referred to initial payments of up to ₲10 million. These are different measures, not contradictory universal payment promises. 1 7 15

On 5 November the BCP announced a tool to identify the institution allocated to pay each depositor's advance. Check the official BCP payment portal for current instructions; the date of an editorial article is not a guarantee that all funds are available. 25

People need access to wages, pensions and money for everyday needs. The BCP intervention team also said it was working on payments due to bank employees. 26

12 / SolutionsA citizen-first response to the crisis

Protecting depositors is not the same as rescuing shareholders at any cost. Within applicable law, a citizen-first response would prioritise:

1. Fast, accessible deposit protection

Publish dates, build secure enquiry channels, and provide non-digital assistance for pensioners, employees and small firms.

2. Independent asset assessment

Audit loan recoverability, security and related-party exposure; determine what can be sold or transferred to sound institutions.

3. Public funds accountability

Publish lawful information on each public entity’s exposure, authorisations, investment criteria and contractual terms.

4. Review of supervision

Identify warnings, decision makers, legal powers, responses and evidence that corrective measures worked.

5. Due process and reform

Preserve evidence, respect defence rights and strengthen related-party, beneficial-ownership and conflict-of-interest controls.

A bank can fail. Accountability and the protection of ordinary people should not fail with it.

Paraguay needs verifiable answers, functioning deposit protection and institutional review that does not mistake outrage for evidence. The question is not only who lost office, but who must account for which decisions and how citizens can recover their money.
