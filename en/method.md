# Method & sources

**The question comes before the conclusion.** We establish what an article is trying to verify, argue or explain before we write it.

## Three editorial formats

- **Verification (`dossier`):** checks claims and documents, separating **VERIFIED**, **REPORTED AND DISPUTED**, and **NOT YET ESTABLISHED**.
- **Opinion (`saggio`):** makes a case, identifies its factual basis, and addresses serious counterarguments.
- **Analysis (`contesto`):** makes a topic understandable, defines technical terms and sets out what is known and unknown.

The subject category and editorial format are independent. Older articles are not assigned a format without editorial review.

## Sources and clarity

Where possible, we examine original records, official statistics, legislation and relevant studies. We check dates, definitions, quotations and methods. A political proposal is not an enacted law; a theoretical projection is not a confirmed result.

Technical terms are explained on first use so that readers with different backgrounds can follow the evidence. **Full references appear at the end of every article.** Material uncertainty is acknowledged, not concealed.

## Transparency and corrections

Historical comparisons are optional; there is no fixed publication schedule. AI may assist preliminary work but is not a substitute for original evidence or human editorial approval. Substantive errors are documented through our [corrections policy]({{ '/en/corrections/' | relative_url }}).
